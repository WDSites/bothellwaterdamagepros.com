Mold in Carpet From Water Damage: A Bothell, WA Guide

Carpet and pad hold water long after the surface feels dry. Here is how to tell whether yours can be saved, and what to do if mold has started.

How carpet gets moldy after water damage

Carpet is a sponge sitting on another sponge. The fibers hold water, the backing holds water, and the pad underneath holds the most. Once wet, a carpet system can stay damp for days while the surface feels fine.

Mold needs moisture, a food source and time. Carpet and pad supply the food, the leak supplies the moisture, and the hours that pass supply the time.

Local note

Wet carpet and the Bothell rain calendar

The Seattle-Tacoma airport normals show 3.91 inches of rain in October, 6.31 in November and 5.72 in December. Those fall and winter months are when carpets in Bothell basements and ground floors are most likely to get wet from exterior leaks.

Public Health Seattle and King County publishes mold guidance for King County residents. Bothell has homes in both King and Snohomish counties, so check the guidance that applies to your address, and consult your physician for health questions.

Source type: Weather-service climate normals and county public health guidance.

The 24 to 48 hour rule

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. The Washington Department of Health gives the same window for carpets, clothing, bedding and upholstered furniture. That window is the reason speed beats everything else in a carpet flood.

If carpet has stayed wet longer, the EPA says to assume mold growth. At that point the question is not whether to throw it out but how to do it safely.

Signs of mold in wet carpet

  • A musty or earthy smell that gets stronger when you step on the carpet.
  • Dark, green, white or gray spots on the carpet surface, backing or tack strip.
  • Staining on the baseboards or the lower drywall near the wet area.
  • Carpet that feels damp days after the water stopped.
  • Ripples or lifting where the pad has soaked and the backing has loosened.

Public Health Seattle and King County advises throwing out wall-to-wall carpet and padding that has been saturated by flood waters. Sewage and flood water carry contaminants that cannot be cleaned out of carpet.

What to do before help arrives

Stop the source

Shut off the water. If it is a sewer backup, stop using the drains.

Pull furniture off the carpet

Foil or plastic under legs keeps rust and stain from spreading.

Extract what you can

A wet vacuum pulls water from the fibers. Work slowly and empty the tank often.

Run air

Open windows if it is dry outside and use fans. Do not use a central air system if the water is dirty.

Get the pad checked

Lift a corner and feel the pad. If it is soaked, it should come out.

Cleaning moldy carpet: when it is a DIY job

The Washington Department of Health defines a small mold area as less than about ten square feet, or a patch three feet by three feet. Public Health Seattle and King County suggests wearing an N-95 mask, gloves and goggles for small cleanups, and getting professional help if the growth covers more than ten square feet.

Carpet is hard to clean once mold is in the backing. Surface cleaning rarely reaches the pad or the subfloor. In most cases the carpet and pad are removed, and the subfloor is cleaned and dried.

What professionals do differently

A crew removes the carpet and pad in sections, bags them where they lie to limit spread and cleans the tack strip and subfloor. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room, and moisture readings confirm the subfloor is dry before any new carpet goes in.

When mold has grown beyond a small area, that work becomes mold remediation, which includes containment and removal of affected material. Ask the crew to explain the scope before they start. Take health questions to your physician.

Why the subfloor matters as much as the carpet

A new carpet over a damp subfloor repeats the problem. Plywood and oriented strand board soak up water, and concrete slabs hold moisture that rises through them. Before replacement, the subfloor should read dry on a moisture meter and smell clean.

If the subfloor shows staining, swelling or surface growth, it may need cleaning, sanding or sealing before the new pad goes down. A water damage cleanup crew can scope that step.

Keeping carpet dry in a wet climate

  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so rain stays away from exterior walls.
  • Use a dehumidifier in basements and ground-floor rooms during the rainy months.
  • Fix sticking doors and windows that let rain blow in.
  • Check carpet edges along exterior walls and under windows after heavy storms.
  • Clean up pet accidents and spills fast, because slow moisture feeds the same problem.

If the carpet needs to come out

Carpet removal and subfloor cleaning fall under water damage cleanup. Growth beyond a small area belongs to mold remediation.
