How Do You Remove Mold From Basement Walls? A Bothell, WA Guide

Concrete, block and drywall need different handling. This guide shows what to clean yourself, what to leave to a crew and how to stop the wall from growing mold again.

Start by finding out why the wall is wet

Mold on a basement wall is a symptom. The cause is moisture, and if the moisture stays, the mold returns after any cleaning. Before you reach for a scrub brush, spend ten minutes working out where the water comes from.

Tape a square of plastic wrap tightly to the wall and leave it for a day. Moisture on the wall side of the plastic means water is moving through the wall. Moisture on the room side means humid indoor air is condensing on a cold surface.

Local note

Low ground and creeks in Bothell

The city's 2025 hazard mitigation annex says 6.10 percent of Bothell's land area is in Special Flood Hazard Areas and describes minor flooding in low-lying spots along North Creek. A basement near the creek is more likely to see groundwater pressure on its walls.

The Snohomish County flood study lists North Creek in Bothell as a Zone AE stream with a floodway. If your address is near it, check the FEMA flood map and ask your insurer, because a typical home policy does not cover flood damage.

Poured concrete or block.Grows on dust and surface film, rarely penetrates deeply.Scrub, rinse, dry fully, then fix the moisture source.
Painted masonry.Lifts the paint and grows under it.Strip loose paint, clean, dry, reseal.

Porous materials such as drywall and insulation are harder to clean than hard ones such as concrete. Once drywall paper is moldy, removal is usually the cleaner answer.

Small cleanups: the state guidance

The Washington Department of Health says a small area of mold is less than about ten square feet, or a patch three feet by three feet. Public Health Seattle and King County advises wearing an N-95 mask, gloves and goggles for small cleanups, drying surfaces quickly and thoroughly after cleaning, and getting professional help if the growth covers more than ten square feet.

The health department also says to hire a professional if the area has been contaminated by sewage or is in hidden places. That includes mold inside a wall cavity.

A careful cleaning sequence for a small patch

Suit up

Wear an N-95 mask, gloves and goggles. Open a window and run a fan that blows out, not in.

Contain the dust

Do not dry-brush the mold, which throws spores into the air. Mist the surface lightly first.

Scrub hard surfaces

Use detergent and water on concrete or block. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.

Wipe and dry

Remove residue with clean damp rags, then dry the wall with fans and a dehumidifier.

Bag and dispose

Seal rags and debris in plastic bags before they leave the basement.

Watch the wall

If growth returns within weeks, the moisture source has not been fixed.

When the job is bigger than a bucket

If the mold covers more than about ten square feet, appears behind drywall or follows a flood, treat it as a professional job. Mold remediation means containing the work area, using negative air and HEPA filtration, removing affected material and verifying that it is dry.

That scope exists because scrubbing in an unsealed basement can spread dust through the whole house. Crews seal the area, so the rest of the home stays clean.

Fixing the moisture is the real job

  • Regrade soil so it slopes away from the foundation.
  • Extend downspouts several feet away from the wall.
  • Seal cracks from the outside when possible.
  • Run a dehumidifier in the basement during the rainy months.
  • Inspect plumbing and water heater connections for slow drips.

If water comes up through the slab or the wall after a storm, stop the water first with emergency water removal and then plan the repairs.

Efflorescence is not mold

White, powdery crust on concrete or block is often efflorescence, a mineral deposit left when water moves through the wall and evaporates. It is not mold, but it is a reliable sign that water is moving through the wall.

Brush it off and treat it as a warning. If it comes back after a dry spell, look for the water source outside the wall. Mold, by contrast, looks fuzzy or spotty, may be green, black, white or gray and often smells musty.

What to keep and what to throw out

  • Cardboard boxes and papers that sat against moldy walls.
  • Upholstered furniture and mattresses that were stored on the wet floor.
  • Wood furniture can often be cleaned and dried, unless it is heavily stained or soft.
  • Plastic, metal and glass items can be washed and kept.
  • Insulation and drywall in contact with mold should be removed.
