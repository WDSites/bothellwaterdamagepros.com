Frozen pipe damage in Bothell: occasional, but real

.

Frozen pipe damage in Bothell happens in cold snaps, not every winter. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Seattle-Tacoma area, the nearest station we could read, show most freezing nights falling in November through March. Those normals list 5.8 days at or below 32 degrees in February, 3.0 in November and 1.9 in March, and no days at or below 0 degrees in any month.

A hard, multi-day freeze is unusual here, and many Bothell homes never see a pipe freeze. The ones that do usually have pipes in unheated places or a house left cold. For the more common causes of indoor flooding, see emergency water removal.

Where pipes freeze in homes here

.

Exterior walls

A kitchen or bathroom sink on an outside wall has a supply line with little insulation between it and the cold. Seattle Public Utilities lists pipes near outer walls as vulnerable.

Crawl spaces and attics

These unheated spaces hold supply lines that sit in cold air for hours. The same utility names crawl spaces and attics among the common freeze points.

Garages

Lines to a garage laundry sink or water heater get no help from the house heat, and an open garage door makes it worse.

Hose bibs

Outdoor faucets are the first to fail. A hose left attached traps water in the faucet and the line behind it.

The thaw is when the leak shows up

.

Ice in a pipe expands and can split the metal or a fitting, but the ice itself plugs the opening. Water often does not escape until the ice melts. That is why a quiet house can suddenly have the sound of running water behind a wall on the day the weather turns.

A trickle or no water at a tap is the usual sign of a frozen pipe. Keep the faucet open and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, heating pad or warm towels. The National Weather Service says never to use an open flame. If the pipe has already split, see burst pipe water damage.

Frozen pipe safety: what to do right now

.

Find the main shut-off

Seattle Public Utilities says the valve is usually in a basement, crawl space or garage. Close it if water is flowing.

Do not use electrical devices in standing water

Seattle Public Utilities warns against electrical devices where there is standing water. Cut power at a dry panel or call your electric utility.

Thaw only a pipe that is not leaking

Use a hair dryer, heating pad or warm towels, never a flame.

Call a plumber for the pipe

The utility says to call a licensed plumber if you cannot find, reach or thaw the frozen section.

Call us for the water

Once the water is off, call the dispatch line for extraction and drying.

Vacation homes and cold garages

.

A second home or a house left empty for a week is where a freeze costs the most, because the leak can run unseen for days. The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says burst-pipe coverage generally applies as long as you heat your home properly. Seattle Public Utilities recommends a thermostat setting no lower than 55 degrees during freezing weather.

Before you leave, disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and open under-sink cabinet doors. Ask a neighbor to look in if a freeze is forecast.

Our process after a freeze split

.

Check the source

Water must be off and a plumber on the repair before drying begins.

Extract and pull wet materials

Pumps and extractors remove water. Wet pad and insulation come out.

Find hidden water

Moisture meters and a thermal camera trace water through wall cavities that look dry.

Dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry framing and subfloor, with readings logged.

Dry targets follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, cited as a reference and not as a certification. Rebuilding is described on water damage repair.

Mistakes to avoid in a cold snap

.
  • Using a torch or other open flame on a frozen pipe.
  • Turning the heat off in a house you are leaving for days.
  • Leaving hoses attached to outdoor faucets.
  • Assuming a pipe that thawed on its own is undamaged.
