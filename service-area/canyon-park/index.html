Where Canyon Park sits along SR-527

.

Canyon Park is a Bothell subarea at the north end of the city. The city's plan puts its northern edge along the city limits on 208th Street SE, also called Filbert Road or Maltby Road, and its western edge near 8th Avenue SE. SR-527, the Bothell-Everett Highway, is the main north-south road through it. The plan states that the subarea is entirely in Snohomish County.

Most of it is not housing. In the city's land-use figures for the area, single-family lots make up about 6 percent of net acreage and multifamily about 6 percent, so only about 12 percent of the land is homes and apartments. The rest is business and mixed-use ground, so homes sit among business parks near SR-527 and the creeks.

ZIP code 98021 is shared with other places, so a ZIP alone does not show whether an address is in this part of Bothell. Ground just north of 208th Street SE is partly unincorporated.

What the city plans say about the water

.

North Creek crosses the middle of the subarea from northwest to southeast. Where it passes under SR-527 it is held in by a levee system on the east side, and the city plan says the stretch just below the levee carries a lot of sediment and is eroding toward Village Square. Perry Creek drains in from the southwest and joins North Creek.

Extensive wetlands across the subarea hold and slow stormwater. About one third of the net acreage, 302 of 934.8 acres, is mapped as critical area, and erosion hazard areas follow steep slopes on the east side, west of SR-527 and near Perry Creek. Flooding here is most often triggered by heavy rain and worsened by development in the basin.

Source type: City of Bothell Canyon Park subarea plan and existing-conditions study.

For a home or apartment, that means saturated soil and slow drainage pushing water toward foundations, garage floors and ground-floor units, while storm rain strains roofs and site drains.

What FEMA maps and past floods say about Canyon Park

.

The city's study says FEMA mapping shows Zone X and Zone AE floodplains along North Creek inside the study area. The Snohomish County flood insurance study lists North Creek within Bothell as Zone AE with a floodway. Those entries describe stream reaches. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

You can look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so ask your insurer what yours says.

December 2007 was a citywide and regional flood, and the sources do not name Canyon Park in it. A January 1986 North Creek berm failure flooded land between the west levee and I-405 with no significant damage reported, and the sources do not place it relative to this subarea. No dated home flooding tied to Canyon Park was found in the public sources checked.

Water problems that fit homes and apartments here

.
  • Ground-floor apartments and condos where a failed water heater or supply line wets the unit and the ceiling below it.
  • Houses near the slopes and wetland edges where seepage collects under the floor, covered on crawl space water damage.
  • Roof, gutter and window leaks after a heavy rain, covered on storm water damage.

Water safety first steps

.
  • Stop the source if it is safe, such as at the fixture valve or main shutoff.
  • Stay out of flooded rooms. Do not touch breakers or electrical items while standing in water, or if water reached outlets or the panel.
  • Creek water and sewage are contaminated, so leave cleanup and drying of those to a crew.
  • Photograph the damage before you move anything, and do not decide yet what is lost or what can be saved.

How a crew works through a wet home

.

A crew extracts standing water, then maps moisture with meters and a thermal camera. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, as described under structural drying. Materials that cannot dry are cut out and the work is documented. If materials stay wet, mold can grow, so drying quickly matters, as covered under mold remediation.

Paperwork for your insurer

.

Keep dated photos, receipts, moisture readings and notes on what was removed. Ask your insurer what applies to you.
