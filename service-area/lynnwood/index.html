Wet-season water damage in Lynnwood, WA

Water damage restoration in Lynnwood, WA gets busiest when the long autumn and winter rains saturate the ground. Our 24/7 dispatch line covers Lynnwood from our Bothell base, and I-405 connects the two cities.

Lynnwood was incorporated in 1959 and sits midway between Seattle and Everett in Snohomish County. Most of its streets were built for cars and lots, with little thought for the creeks underneath. The city's own flood studies explain why some of those creeks still spill in a big storm.

Scriber Creek and the 44th Avenue West underpass

The city's Scriber Creek advisory committee reports repeated and significant flooding between 188th and 196th Street SW over about 20 years. Specific events include water above the finished floor at 190th Street SW and 55th Avenue W in 2006. A December 2007 flood overtopped an access road and flooded the first floor of an apartment building. A November 2012 flood put a garage at 189th Street SW under water.

The causes the committee lists are development, a confined channel, undersized culverts and sediment. The city is now building the 188th Flood Wall along Scriber Creek, and its 2025 plan notes about half a mile of floodplain right next to the City Center light rail station.

For a resident, the lesson is concrete. Low garages, ground-floor units and crawl spaces near this corridor can take water even when your street looks dry. If you live near it, check the FEMA flood map and ask your insurer about flood coverage.

Apartments and condos from the 1960s and 1970s

The city's housing needs assessment says 49 percent of Lynnwood's homes are single-family and 46 percent of households rent. Of the new units built from 2013 to 2018, 86 percent were multifamily. The City Center plan adds that low-rise apartments west of 48th Avenue W date from the 1960s and 1970s, while newer buildings have opened near the station.

Older low-rise buildings tend to have aged supply lines, roofs and drain stacks, and newer midrise buildings tend to have water-heater and appliance failures. In either, water runs down through ceilings into the unit below. Tell your property manager at once, call your insurer, and let us document each unit it touched.

When water is spreading in Lynnwood

The next ten minutes matter more than the next ten hours. Work through these only as far as is safe.

Stop the flow

Close the fixture valve or the main valve. In an apartment, find the unit shutoff before the building's.

Leave electrical hazards alone

If water reaches outlets or a panel, step back and call 911 and your utility before you call us.

Raise what you can

Move electronics, shoes, books and paperwork off the floor onto counters or beds.

Avoid dirty water

Creek and sewer water can carry bacteria. Keep children and pets away, and wash up after contact.

Record the damage

Take photos and video of the source and every wet surface before anything is thrown out.

Who handles water and sewer in Lynnwood

The City of Lynnwood Public Works Department runs the city's sewer and water maintenance and its wastewater treatment plant. The city buys Everett water through Alderwood Water and Wastewater District, and the city's water plan lists 481 direct district accounts inside the city limits. Your bill shows which one serves you.

For a sewer backup, the city's page separates what the city handles from what the homeowner handles. A blocked private line is a plumber's job. The sewage that has reached your floors is Category 3 water, and it needs extraction, removal of porous materials and disinfection.

Permits and the Lynnwood Permit Center

Development and Business Services issues building, electrical and other permits from the Permit Center at 19100 44th Ave W. The city says a remodel typically needs a building permit and that bigger projects beyond a simple repair may need more. Painting is exempt.

Ask the Permit Center how the rules apply to your repair before you rebuild walls, move plumbing or touch wiring.
