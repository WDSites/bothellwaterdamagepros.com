Where Brickyard Road and Queensgate sits in southeast Bothell

.

The City of Bothell's subarea plan places Brickyard Road/Queensgate in Bothell's southeastern corner, on about 370 acres inside city limits. SR-522 forms the north edge, 124th Avenue NE the east edge, the Tolt River Pipe Line the south edge and I-405 the west edge. The east boundary is Woodinville's city limit, so this area meets Woodinville and not Kenmore.

The plan describes most of the area as single family homes at varying densities, with a minimum detached lot of 9,600 sq ft. Plats it names include Totem Estates, Queensgate Nos. 3 and 5, and Hillside Estates. Multifamily pockets and some commercial and light industrial land near Brickyard Road and East Riverside Drive share the same streets.

What the city's subarea plan says about the ground and the water

.

The plan says steep slopes of more than 15 percent occupy the majority of the northern part of the area and are classified a potential landslide hazard. Ground drops from about 340 feet to about 18 feet at the Sammamish River. On a hillside lot, water moves downhill through soil, so a basement wall can seep after long rain.

A large wetland extends from NE 160th Street to the Tolt River Pipe Line right-of-way. A stream from a wetland near NE 164th Place and 122nd Avenue NE runs northwest to the Sammamish River and is largely piped. The plan also says a one percent chance flood plain, the 100-year floodplain, surrounds the Sammamish River between SR-522 and East Riverside Drive/Woodinville Drive.

Source type: the City of Bothell Brickyard Road/Queensgate subarea plan and its December 2024 summary.

Safety before anyone starts cleaning up

.
  • Stop the source if you can do it safely, such as the main water valve.
  • Do not enter flooded areas, and do not operate breakers or touch electrical while standing in water or if water reached outlets or the panel.
  • Sewage and creek water are contaminated, so leave cleanup and drying of those to professionals.
  • Photograph the damage before you move things.
  • Do not assume your belongings are all lost, and do not assume all of them can be kept.

How a crew handles a wet home here

.

A crew pulls out standing water first, then maps where moisture went with meters and a thermal camera, since water often travels behind baseboards and under flooring. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, as described on structural drying.

Materials that cannot dry, such as soaked pad or swollen drywall, are removed. If materials stay wet, mold can grow, so drying quickly matters, and mold remediation is a separate scope. Readings and removals are written down as the job goes.

Documenting a loss for your insurer

.
