When water is spreading in Kenmore

Water that keeps moving costs more every hour. Do these things while you wait for a crew, and only the ones that are safe.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main water valve. In many homes the main sits where the line enters the house, often in a garage, basement or crawl space.

Mind the electricity

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If water touches outlets, a panel or a gas appliance, leave and call 911 and your utility first.

Lift what you can

Move electronics, rugs and small furniture onto dry ground. Leave heavy furniture and wet drywall alone.

Keep people and pets clear

Sewage and creek water carry bacteria. Keep children and animals out of it, and wash your hands after any contact.

Take photos first

Photograph the water, the damaged items and the source before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

Sewage backup in Kenmore: who to call first

Northshore Utility District provides water and sewer service in Kenmore, according to the city's utilities page. The district says most sewer backups start in the private plumbing system, meaning the pipe on your property rather than the main in the street.

That split decides who you call. If the problem is in the street main, the utility's 24-hour sewer backup line, listed on its sewer backups page, is the right call. If it is in your own line, a plumber clears it. Either way, the sewage already inside is Category 3 water. It needs extraction, removal of porous materials and disinfection, and that part is ours.

The district also notes that work on a private sewer line needs a permit, so ask before anyone digs.
