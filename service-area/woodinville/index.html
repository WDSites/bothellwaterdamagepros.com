What water damage looks like in Woodinville, WA

Water damage restoration in Woodinville, WA usually starts with one of a short list of causes. A failed supply line or water heater, a roof or window leak, a drain that backed up, or creek and culvert water after heavy rain are the common ones. The 24/7 dispatch line covers Woodinville from our Bothell base.

Woodinville covers 5.65 square miles at the King and Snohomish county line. The Sammamish River forms much of its east edge, and the Tourist District and NE 145th Street (SR 202) mark the south. ZIP 98072 reaches across the county line, so a Woodinville mailing address is not always a King County address. Ask which county and which utility serve your lot before you call anyone about permits or flood maps.

Creeks, culverts and the 2007 flood

King County's 2025 hazard annex says flooding has occurred along the banks of the Sammamish River and Little Bear Creek, mostly because of blocked and aging culverts. About 1.7 percent of the city lies in the 100-year floodplain. The county counts three structures there, two residential and one commercial, plus five repetitive-loss properties.

The most recent flood the annex lists happened in 2007, when a tree jammed a culvert under SR 522. The lesson for a homeowner is that one clogged culvert can flood a street that no map marks as a flood zone. If water is pooling near a culvert or storm drain, report it to the city, then call us about whatever reached the house.

Creek and river water is Category 3. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so check your policy and the FEMA flood map for your address.

Newer buildings, different leaks

The city's 2023 community profile says only 20 percent of Woodinville's housing was built before 1970 and about 15 percent has been built since 2010. Detached single-family homes make up 55 percent of the stock and multifamily nearly 39 percent. Woodin Creek Village alone added more than 500 downtown units.

That mix changes the leaks. Newer houses tend to lose water from supply lines, water heaters, ice maker lines and appliance hoses rather than old pipe. In multifamily buildings water travels between floors, so a leak in one unit shows up as a stained ceiling in the one below. Call your HOA or property manager and your insurer, and we will document the wet rooms on every floor we can reach.

Single-family homes

Look first at the water heater closet, laundry room, kitchen sink and upstairs bathrooms. Check the attic after a heavy rain.

Condos and apartments

Water usually enters from above or from a shared wall. Document each affected unit before anything is dried or removed.

Commercial buildings

The city counts more than 130 wineries, breweries and distilleries. A leak near stock or equipment needs fast written documentation, and the same line takes commercial calls.

Hillside lots

Steep slopes sit west and east of the city. Runoff from higher ground flows toward the valley floor and can find a foundation crack.

Snow, wind and power outages in Woodinville

The county annex records two consecutive snowstorms in February 2019 that totaled up to 15 inches in some neighborhoods. City Hall closed for two days and schools for more than a week. It also says windstorms cause outages in parts of the city two or three times a year, with many trees and overhead lines involved.

Melting roof snow can find a weak flashing and leak into an attic days later. Outages stop sump pumps. After a storm, look at ceilings under the roof line, and if your power was off for hours, check the lowest floor before you go to bed.

When water is spreading in Woodinville

Start with the water you can see, then work outward. These steps are in a safe order.

Find where it enters

Decide whether the water comes from a pipe, the roof, a drain or the outside. A pipe can be shut off. A flooded yard cannot.

Shut off the right valve

Turn off the fixture valve or the main water valve. Do not touch the valve if you have to stand in water to reach it.

Electricity comes first

If water is near outlets, a panel or the water heater, stay out and call 911 and your utility before calling us.

Protect what is dry

Lift electronics, books and rugs onto dry furniture. Pull curtains up off wet floors.

Document before cleanup

Take photos and a short video of every wet room, the source and damaged belongings.
