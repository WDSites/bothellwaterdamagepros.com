How water gets into Bothell basements

.

A basement is a hole in wet ground, so water finds it through more routes than any other room. Rain pushes through the joint where the floor meets the wall. Window wells fill when their drains clog. A sump pump quits in a storm, and a sewer line can also back up through a floor drain. A pipe can burst above and run down the stairs.

This page covers basements that are partly or fully below grade, including finished ones with carpet and drywall. Rooms flooded by a river or creek belong to flood damage restoration. The space under a raised floor is covered on crawl space water damage, and the sewage case has its own page, sewage backup cleanup.

What the geology and the insurer say

.

A USGS study of East King County describes Vashon glacial till, often called hardpan, as a compact mix of sand, gravel and boulders in silt and clay that acts as a confining layer, with water able to perch in its upper part. The study does not name Bothell, so read it as the regional setting and not a reading for your lot.

The Washington insurance commissioner lists sewer backup and sump pump overflow as optional endorsements to ask your agent about. It also says water seeping in from the ground and a leaking foundation are not usually covered. Call your insurer before you assume either way.

Basement safety: the first hour

.
  • Do not walk into standing water if the basement has outlets, a panel or a sump. Electricity and water together can kill. If the panel is in a wet basement, call your power utility or 911.
  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source.
  • Keep children and pets upstairs.
  • Lift valuables, photograph the water level on the walls, and call the 24/7 line.

Cleanup and drying sequence

.

Stop or identify the source

A pipe is shut off. Seepage and sump failure are noted, because the cause decides what gets dried and how often water returns.

Extract the water

A submersible pump lowers deep water, then extraction units lift water out of carpet and off the slab.

Remove what cannot dry

Wet carpet pad, baseboard and the lower part of drywall are cut out. Insulation behind the drywall is removed with it.

Map and measure

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how high the water wicked. The slab and the bottom plates of the walls are checked.

Dry with dehumidifiers

LGR dehumidifiers and air movers run in a closed room with the outside door shut. Concrete dries slowly, so the process is on structural drying and dehumidification.

Confirm

Readings are logged each visit. Rebuilding waits until the framing and slab read dry.

Finished basements: carpet, drywall and trim

.

A finished basement hides water well. Water travels behind baseboard and under the bottom plate, so a wall can look fine while the cavity is wet. We check the cavity before closing anything up, and cut a clean opening rather than patching a wet wall.

Wet carpet over a concrete slab can sometimes be dried in place if the water was clean and caught quickly. Pad is usually removed. Drywall that stays wet loses strength and grows mold, so the EPA's line is to dry water-damaged material within 24 to 48 hours.

Avoiding a repeat

.
  • Clear window well drains and covers before the first fall storm.
  • Test the sump by pouring water into the pit and make sure the float lifts.
  • Consider a battery backup for the sump if the basement is finished.
  • Look for repeating damp lines on walls after rain. They show where the water enters.

Insurers want the loss reported quickly, and delays can cost you coverage. We document water levels and affected materials with photographs. Your policy decides coverage.
