About the service

Water damage restoration in Bothell, WA, explained

Water damage restoration in Bothell, WA is the full process of stopping water, removing it, drying the structure, cleaning what it touched and rebuilding what could not be saved. A burst supply line, a failed water heater, a roof leak and a backed-up drain can all start it, and the first day decides how much of the house survives.

Bothell is a city of about 52,000 people that straddles King and Snohomish counties along the Sammamish River, and its wet season is long. Even the nearest long-term weather station, at Seattle-Tacoma airport, averages 39.34 inches of rain a year, with November the wettest month. That is why emergency calls cluster in fall and winter.

If water is moving through your home, call first and read afterward. The 24/7 line is staffed all night, and the dispatcher will tell you what to shut off, what to lift and what to leave alone while a crew is on the way.

Know before you call

What shapes a water damage bill

We do not quote a price over the phone before we see the damage, because the same leak can be a small job or a large one. These five factors move the bill most.

Water category

Clean, gray and sewage water need different handling, and contaminated water costs more to remove and disinfect.

Affected area

The number of wet rooms and square feet sets how much equipment runs and for how long.

Materials involved

Carpet, hardwood, drywall and plaster dry at different speeds and some cannot be saved.

Drying days

Moisture readings decide how long drying lasts, not a calendar.

Rebuild scope

Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint is priced apart from drying.

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says home policies are meant for sudden disasters. Most do not cover gradual leaks, and a typical policy does not cover flooding. The agency also says you may choose your own contractor and that an insurer has 30 days from the date you file to inspect the damage.

Report the loss quickly, follow your insurer's instructions for stopping the water and ask your insurer what your policy covers. If you are unsure whether a loss is sudden or gradual, say so on the call and describe what you saw.
