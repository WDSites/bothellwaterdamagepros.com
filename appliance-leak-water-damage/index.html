Appliance leaks are quiet, and the floor pays for it

.

Appliance leak water damage comes from the machines that use water every day: a washing machine, a dishwasher, a refrigerator with an ice maker, and the utility sink and laundry drains. These leaks start small. The appliance hides the spot, the finish floor hides the wet subfloor, and nobody sees anything until the damage is advanced.

This page covers the appliance and the floor beneath it. Cabinets and kitchen finishes are on kitchen water damage, and a leaking tank is on water heater leak cleanup.

Where each appliance fails

.

Washing machine

The supply hoses are under pressure whenever the taps behind the machine are open, and the drain hose can pop out of the standpipe during a spin cycle. A second-floor laundry room leaks into the ceiling below.

Dishwasher

The inlet line, the door gasket and the drain connection are the weak points. A leak shows first at the toe-kick, then in the cabinet floor and the flooring beside it.

Refrigerator ice maker

A thin supply line behind the fridge can crack or pull off a fitting. It can drip for weeks, soaking the subfloor under the whole refrigerator.

Utility sink and drains

A loose trap, a clogged drain or a sump pump discharge line that backs up can overflow. Water that comes up from a floor drain is a sewage matter, covered under sewage backup cleanup.

Slow leaks versus sudden ones

.

A sudden failure is a hose that bursts or a machine that overflows, and the room is wet within the hour. The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner gives a dishwasher that overflows during a wash and an ice-maker line that snaps and sprays water over the kitchen floor as examples of sudden leaks that are usually covered if reported quickly.

A slow leak is the bigger risk to the building. The same office says insurers treat problems like these as maintenance, not sudden accidents, and that damage developing over more than a couple of days is generally not covered. A hose upgrade is cheap protection, and the office recommends braided stainless steel hoses over rubber.

Appliance leak safety: first steps

.

Stop the water

Close the small valve behind the washer, under the dishwasher or behind the refrigerator. If you cannot reach it, close the main shut-off.

Unplug only if the floor is dry

Do not reach for a cord while standing in water. If the outlet is wet, switch off that circuit at a dry panel or call your electric utility.

Keep the machine from running

Do not use the appliance again until a technician has repaired the line.

Check the meter if unsure

Northshore Utility District says to note the meter reading, wait one or two hours with no water use, and read it again.

Why a hidden leak rots the subfloor

.

Under vinyl, laminate or tile sits a plywood or composite subfloor. When an appliance leaks onto it, the wood absorbs water and the finish above hides it. Wood that stays wet over days softens and eventually decays, and the same moisture can reach joists and the ceiling below.

The Census Bureau puts Bothell's owner-occupied housing rate at 65.5 percent, so many households rent. If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager in writing the same day and keep your own photographs.

Our process for an appliance leak

.

Locate the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far water traveled under the appliance.

Pull the appliance, open the toe-kick

Trim comes off so air can reach the subfloor.

Lift wet flooring

Pad, laminate or sections of vinyl come up where they cannot dry in place.

Dry and verify

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged until dry targets are met.

Dry targets follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, cited as a reference and not as a certification. The rebuild follows on water damage repair.

Prevention that actually helps

.
  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless steel ones.
  • Shut off the washer valves when you go out of town.
  • Check behind the refrigerator and under the dishwasher a few times a year.
  • Do not run the washer or dishwasher while you sleep or are out.
  • Put a leak sensor on the floor beside the machine.
