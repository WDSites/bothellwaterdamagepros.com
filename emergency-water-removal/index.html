Standing water in your Bothell home: do these four things first

Emergency water removal in Bothell, WA is for water deep enough to pump or vacuum right now. That includes a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a flooded basement or crawl space and a washing machine that ran all night. The 24/7 line is open, and the minutes before a crew arrives are worth using.

How standing water is removed

Depth decides the equipment. Submersible pumps handle water that is several inches deep and pull it down until a floor is exposed. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units then lift water out of carpet, pad and the surface of hard flooring. Weighted extraction wands press water out of carpet and wood without tearing it up.

Extraction is only the first half. Water left in the subfloor and wall cavities keeps spreading after the visible pool is gone, so a crew moves straight to drying. That handoff is covered on our water mitigation page.

Burst pipe water damage

Cold snaps are the usual cause of winter burst pipes. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Seattle-Tacoma area average 25.2 days a year at or below 32 degrees, with 7.2 in December and 7.0 in January. These are regional figures, but they show that a freeze is a regular event here rather than a rare one.

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says policies generally cover burst pipes, including frozen pipes, as long as you heat your home properly. A slow drip that went unnoticed for weeks is treated differently, because most policies do not cover gradual leaks. If a pipe lets go, close the main valve first, then open a low faucet to drain what is left in the line, and call us about the water on the floor.

Water in a crawl space or garage

Callers often find water in a crawl space days after it started. The Washington insurance commissioner's advice for homeowners is to check the crawl space regularly for dampness, moldy smells, sagging insulation and moisture on the vapor barrier. That same list tells you what a crew checks after the pumping is done.

Wet insulation hangs low and holds moisture against the floor above, so it usually comes out. We also look at the subfloor from below and read moisture levels before anything is closed up.

Downtown, Canyon Park and North Creek: what changes by neighborhood

The city's comprehensive plan says most of Bothell's larger multifamily buildings are in Downtown, so a burst pipe there often reaches more than one unit. Document the water in your unit, tell your property manager and keep the shutoff location handy.

The Canyon Park plan notes extensive wetlands in that subarea and says North Creek is confined by a levee system on the east side. Low ground near those wetlands can hold water longer after a storm, so ask us to check the lowest level of the building as well.
