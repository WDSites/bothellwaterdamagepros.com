Water damage cleanup in Bothell, WA, once the water has stopped

Water damage cleanup in Bothell, WA is the work that begins when the source is stopped and the standing water is gone. It means removing wet carpet pad, soaked drywall, insulation and debris, then cleaning, sanitizing and deodorizing what stays. The first 48 hours matter, and our line is open 24/7.

While you wait for a crew, keep people and pets out of the wet area, do not use a household vacuum on wet floors, and photograph the damage before you move anything. If the water came from a toilet or a drain, assume it is contaminated.

What gets thrown away and what gets saved

Public Health Seattle and King County advises throwing out wall-to-wall carpet and padding saturated by floodwater, throwing away wet drywall and cutting out wet insulation. Clean-water losses are more forgiving, because the Washington Department of Health says carpets, clothing, bedding and upholstered furniture can be saved if they are cleaned and dried within 24 to 48 hours.

Sanitizing and deodorizing

Once the porous materials that cannot be saved are out, surfaces get cleaned with detergent and then treated with an antimicrobial product. HEPA vacuums pick up fine debris, and air scrubbers pull particles out of the air while work continues.

Odor comes from damp materials, not just from visible dirt. We remove the source first, because masking a smell on a wet wall only hides a problem that will return. The CDC advises washing the area with soap and clean water as soon as possible after floodwater and throwing away items that cannot be disinfected, such as wallcoverings, cloth, rugs and drywall.

Check older homes before anything is torn out

Cleanup often means pulling out ceiling texture, flooring and wall material. The Puget Sound Clean Air Agency tells people to check for asbestos before any renovation project. It says homeowners who own and live in a single-family house may survey the house themselves, while renters, landlords and condo owners must hire an AHERA-certified inspector.

The EPA adds that contractors working on pre-1978 homes must be certified and follow lead-safe work practices. Tell the dispatcher your home's age when you call, and ask the agency whether your project needs a notice.

Contents, photos and the insurance record

Photograph every room before cleanup starts, then list damaged items with a rough age and value. Keep a small piece of a damaged material if your insurer may want to see it. We write down what was removed, what was cleaned and what readings were taken.

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says many home policies offer limited mold coverage, and only when the mold results from a covered loss. That is one more reason to start cleanup quickly and keep records.

Cleanup in condos and multifamily buildings

The Bothell comprehensive plan says Downtown is where most of the city's larger multifamily buildings are concentrated. In those buildings water travels through shared walls and ceilings, so a cleanup in your unit may involve the unit above and the one below.

Tell your property manager or HOA board in writing, keep their reference number, and let us document each affected unit. Condo owners should also keep in mind the asbestos rule above before any demolition.

Where cleanup fits in the full job

Cleanup sits between drying and rebuilding. If water is still standing, start with emergency water removal. If the structure is still wet, water mitigation comes first. When the building is clean and dry, water damage repair replaces what was removed.
