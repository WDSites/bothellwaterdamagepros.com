Six services, one 24/7 line

Water damage is rarely one job. It starts with water that has to be stopped and pumped out, moves to drying that follows moisture readings, and ends with cleaning and repair. Bothell Water Damage Pros organizes that sequence into six services so you can find the stage you are in.

Every service is reached through the same dispatch line, (425) 592-3399, which is answered 24/7. You do not need to know which service you need before you call. Describe what you see, and the dispatcher will help sort the first steps.

Water Damage Repair

Rebuilding drywall, ceilings, flooring and paint once the structure is dry.

Water Mitigation

Stopping the spread and drying framing, subfloors and contents in the first days.

Water Damage Cleanup

Removing wet materials, then cleaning, sanitizing and deodorizing.

Emergency Water Removal

Pumps and extraction units for standing water from pipes, heaters and floods.

Flood Damage Restoration

Storm, creek, groundwater and sewage losses handled as contaminated water.

Mold Remediation

Finding the moisture, containing the area and removing affected material.

How the services fit together

Think of the work in three phases. The first phase is emergency response, which covers emergency water removal and water mitigation. Standing water comes out first, then drying equipment goes to work on the materials that soaked it up.

The second phase is cleanup. Water damage cleanup removes what cannot be dried, such as saturated carpet pad or swollen drywall, and treats the surfaces that stay. When the water came from a storm, a creek or a sewer line, flood damage restoration adds protective gear and stricter disposal.

The third phase is rebuild. Water damage repair replaces drywall, flooring, trim and paint after the structure is confirmed dry. Mold remediation sits beside all three, because mold can follow any wet material left damp.

Why Bothell homes see these losses

Bothell sits along the Sammamish River in both King and Snohomish counties, and the Seattle-Tacoma airport weather station records about 39 inches of rain in an average year. Fall and winter bring the heaviest rain, so roof leaks, backed-up drains and basement seepage cluster then.

Burst supply lines, failed water heaters and washing machine hoses can strike in any season. The common thread is speed. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so the first call matters more than the first repair.
