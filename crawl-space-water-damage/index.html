What crawl space water damage looks like under a Bothell house

.

Crawl space water damage is water standing on the ground under a house, soaking the insulation between the joists, or pooling on and under the plastic ground cover. It is found late, because nobody looks there. A damp smell in the hallway, a cold soft floor or a duct hanging loose are often the first clues.

We have no count of Bothell houses that sit on a crawl space and will not guess one. The problem is seasonal. It shows up once the wet months begin and rain, a high water table or a plumbing leak puts water where the soil cannot shed it. The first question is whether the water came from above or from the ground.

This page covers the space under the house. Pumping rooms is on emergency water removal, and a flooded finished lower level is on basement water damage cleanup.

What the sources say about water under houses

.

The EPA's mold course says soil wicks moisture by capillary action, that ground water may enter a crawl space where the water table is high, and that downspouts and landscaping can direct water into or under a building.

King County's Sammamish River valley groundwater study measured a shallow water table that rises and falls about 2 to 4 feet over a year, driven by rain, recharge and river height. Its wells were in Woodinville, not Bothell, so read it as a valley pattern and not a reading for your lot.

Source type: federal EPA training material and a King County groundwater study.

Crawl space safety: before anyone goes in

.

Washington's labor agency lists crawl spaces among spaces that can meet the definition of a confined space, with limited entry and exit and not built to be occupied. That is a reason for homeowners to be careful.

  • Do not enter with water on the ground and wiring, a sump or a light in the space. The CDC says never to turn power on or off while standing in water, so cut power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • If you smell gas, leave, open windows if you can and call the gas utility from outside. A furnace or water heater may sit in or near the space.
  • Never run a gas generator, pump or heater in the house or crawl space, because carbon monoxide builds up.
  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking. Photograph from the access hatch and leave wet insulation where it is.

How we handle a wet crawl space

.

Check the hazards

We look at the electrical, gas appliances and access before anyone enters.

Find the source

A moisture meter and a visual check separate a supply leak from ground water. A plumber fixes a leak, and we handle what it wet.

Pump and remove

A small submersible pump lowers the water. Soaked insulation and debris are bagged out through the hatch.

Map the moisture

Meters on joists and subfloor, and a thermal camera from the room above, show how far the water travelled.

Dry the space

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the wood reads dry. Cold crawl spaces slow refrigerant units, so a desiccant unit may be used, as explained on structural drying and dehumidification.

Wet fiberglass is usually replaced, and replacing it lets us see the underside of the floor. Ducts that touched water are checked for tears. Rodent nests and droppings are reported, and pest control stays with a specialist. Mold on joists is a separate scope, handled through mold remediation after the water is gone.

Insurance questions for a wet crawl space

.

The Washington insurance commissioner says water seeping in from the ground, a leaking foundation and damage that builds over more than a couple of days are usually not covered, and that delaying repairs can cost you coverage. A burst supply line is treated differently from soil that stayed wet all winter. Your policy decides coverage, so ask your insurer.

Preventing the next wet winter

.
  • Point gutters and downspouts away from the foundation.
  • Look through the hatch in October and again in January.
  • Keep the ground cover lapped and intact.
  • Test any sump before the rain starts.
