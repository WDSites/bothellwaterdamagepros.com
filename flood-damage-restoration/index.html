When storm, creek or ground water is the problem

Flood damage restoration in Bothell, WA covers water that comes from outside the plumbing: a storm that overwhelms drains, a creek that spills, or groundwater pushing through a foundation. This water is usually Category 3, so it needs protective gear, removal of porous materials and disinfection. Our 24/7 line is open, and the first calls are about safety.

While you wait for a crew

  • Stay out of the water if you can. It may hide sharp debris and may carry sewage.
  • Do not turn on electrical panels or touch outlets in a flooded space. Call your utility or 911.
  • If the basement is flooded, do not drain it faster than the water outside is falling.
  • Wear boots and gloves, and keep children and pets away.

Why Bothell floods the way it does

Bothell sits along the Sammamish River and straddles King and Snohomish counties. King County says a US Army Corps of Engineers flood control project completed in 1966 channeled the whole river, and that the river generally stays within its banks downstream of Woodinville. A 2002 county plan puts the floodplain at about 1,000 feet wide near Bothell and notes that the channel was never designed to control winter flooding.

The city's 2025 hazard plan says 6.10 percent of Bothell's land lies in special flood hazard areas, including 3.39 percent in Zone X protected by a levee. It lists December 2007 as the most recent significant flood, with heavy rain from December 1 to 17, and says minor flooding happens in low-lying spots along North Creek. It also lists zero repetitive-loss properties.

North Creek, past floods and what they mean now

The Snohomish County flood insurance study, revised June 19, 2020, covers 3.1 miles of North Creek inside Bothell from the county line to 208th Street SE and maps it as Zone AE with a floodway. It records floods in March 1950, December 1975 and January 18, 1986. In 1986 a berm gave way and flooded the area between the west levee and I-405, with no significant damage reported.

That history is modest, but the creek is mapped for a reason. If your home is near North Creek, check the FEMA flood map for your address, because the answer decides whether flood insurance matters. The Washington insurance commissioner says a typical home policy does not cover flooding.

Basement flooding cleanup

Public Health Seattle and King County gives specific advice for flooded basements. Do not drain water inside the basement until most of the water on the outside of the walls has gone down, and pump at the same rate that the flood waters recede. Pumping too fast can put pressure on walls and floors. Electric sump pumps stop working if the panel floods.

The same agency says to start drying the basement as quickly as possible to limit wood decay and mold. We pump in stages, remove saturated carpet and pad, cut out wet drywall and insulation, then dry the framing and slab. Wet basements that are never fully dried are where mold often starts.

Sewage backup cleanup

A sewage backup is a Category 3 loss. The EPA warns that flood water may contain high levels of raw sewage or other hazardous substances, and the CDC says to throw away items that cannot be disinfected, such as wall coverings, cloth, rugs and drywall.

In Bothell, find out who provides your sewer service before you call about the line. The city says several districts operate in and around Bothell, and Northshore Utility District serves parts of the city. A blocked line is a plumber's job. Removing and disinfecting what the sewage touched is ours.

Slopes and the rainy season

The city's hazard plan says about 7.88 percent of Bothell's land is in potential landslide areas and that the southwest portion, mainly south of SR 522 and west of I-405, sees localized landslides semi-regularly in the rainy season. It names Valhalla, Regents Wood, Amber Ridge, Norway Hill, Brentwood and Queensborough among the neighborhoods affected in the past.

Water running down a slope can reach a foundation wall or a crawl space even when no river is involved. If soil is moving, call 911 and leave the building. Otherwise tell the dispatcher about the slope when you call.

Permits in flood areas

Bothell's municipal code requires a development permit before construction or development begins inside any area of special flood hazard, and it is separate from other permits. The public works director administers that article. If your rebuild sits inside a mapped flood area, ask the city before you start.

For an urgent loss, drying and removal begin first. The permit question comes before the rebuild, not before the emergency.
