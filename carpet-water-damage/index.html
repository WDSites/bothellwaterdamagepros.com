Carpet water damage in Bothell: restore it or replace it

.

Carpet water damage restoration in Bothell, WA comes down to one decision, whether the carpet and pad can be saved. The answer depends on the kind of water, how long it sat and what is underneath. Carpet is just the top layer of a stack that also includes the pad and the subfloor.

This page covers that decision. General removal of wet materials is covered by water damage cleanup, and the full drying scope is under water mitigation.

The EPA's guidance for water damage lists carpet and backing as dry within 24 to 48 hours, using a water extraction vacuum, a dehumidifier and fans. It also says mold may grow even when materials are dried within 48 hours. For flood water, the county health department's guidance is that saturated wall-to-wall carpet and its pad should be discarded and not saved.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

.

Do this before the crew arrives

  • Stop the source. Close the valve or have a plumber repair the line.
  • Turn off power to the room if outlets or cords are near the wet carpet.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet. Do not walk on it more than needed, because pressure spreads water into the pad.
  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic so furniture stains do not bleed into the carpet.
  • If the water came from a toilet, drain or outside, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

What lies under the carpet

.

Pad is the weak link. It soaks up water and dries slowly under a sealed carpet. In many jobs the pad comes out while the carpet is lifted, dried and put back.

The subfloor matters just as much. A concrete slab holds moisture differently from plywood on joists. If the subfloor stays wet, carpet put back over it will smell and stain, so we read the subfloor with a moisture meter before the carpet goes down.

Test the water and the carpet

We note the water category and the age and type of carpet and pad.

Extract

Extractors pull water from the carpet and pad, and repeat passes lift more than one pass.

Lift and separate

The carpet is detached from the tack strip, the pad is removed if it cannot be dried, and the carpet is propped to allow airflow.

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the carpet, the subfloor and the lower wall. A thermal camera shows wet areas that the eye misses.

Clean and deodorize

Carpet saved from a clean-water loss is cleaned after it is dry. An antimicrobial product may be used, but it does not replace drying.

Reinstall or replace

The carpet is stretched back or new carpet and pad is installed after readings are dry.

Wall-to-wall carpet versus area rugs

.

Wall-to-wall carpet is tacked down over pad and is harder to dry in place. An area rug can be lifted, rinsed and dried flat in a day or two, so a rug can often be saved even when the carpet under it cannot.

Wool, silk and hand-knotted rugs can bleed dye when wet, so do not leave them damp on a hard floor. Moving them out of the wet room early is one of the best things you can do.

Insurance and mold as a follow-on

.

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says home policies are meant for sudden disasters and may not cover leaks that went on for a long time. Report a loss quickly and keep the wet carpet or a sample if your insurer asks. We cannot promise coverage.

If carpet stayed wet and a musty smell remains after drying, mold may follow. See mold remediation for that scope. For the cause of the water, see flood damage restoration when it came from outside.

Mistakes to avoid

.
  • Leaving the pad under dried carpet.
  • Using only fans with no dehumidifier.
  • Waiting several days to see if it dries on its own.
