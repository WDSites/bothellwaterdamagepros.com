Mold or mildew: does the name matter

People use mold and mildew as if they are different problems. In practice, mildew usually means a flat, powdery or downy growth on a surface, often white or gray, and mold usually means a thicker, fuzzier or darker growth that can go deeper. Both are fungi. Both need moisture.

For cleaning purposes the useful question is not what to call it but how deep it goes, how big it is and what it is growing on.

Local note

Mold season and shared buildings in Bothell

Seattle-Tacoma normals show 6.31 inches of rain in November, 5.78 in January and 5.72 in December. Months of wet weather keep exterior walls, window frames and bathrooms damp, which is when mold and mildew show up.

Bothell's comprehensive plan notes that most of the city's larger multifamily buildings are in Downtown. In such buildings, a moisture problem often starts in one unit and reaches another, so tell your landlord or association early.

Safety for small jobs

Public Health Seattle and King County advises an N-95 mask, gloves and goggles for small cleanups, and says to dry surfaces quickly and thoroughly afterward. It defines a small area as less than about ten square feet, or a patch three feet by three feet.

Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. Open windows or run an exhaust fan, and keep children and pets out of the room. If you have health concerns, consult your physician.

A cleaning routine that works

Fix the moisture

A cleaned surface that stays damp grows mold again.

Protect yourself

Mask, gloves, goggles and a fan blowing outward.

Mist, do not dry brush

A light mist keeps spores from flying.

Scrub with detergent

Work from the edges inward and rinse with clean water.

Dry it completely

Fans and a dehumidifier for at least a day.

Bag the rags

Seal and discard rags and sponges.

When it is time to bring in a pro

  • The growth covers more than about ten square feet.
  • It came after a flood or sewage backup.
  • It is behind walls, under flooring or in the HVAC system.
  • The area returns after cleaning.
  • Someone in the home has health concerns and needs a physician's guidance.

A mold remediation crew contains the area, filters the air, removes affected material and verifies that the area is dry. The scope is written down so you can follow it.

Why mold comes back

The EPA says the key to mold control is moisture control. Mold returns when humidity stays high, when a pipe or roof leaks, or when a building holds water in a hidden cavity. Every repeat of the problem is a message about the moisture source.

Check bathroom fans, dryer vents, window seals and plumbing. Keep indoor humidity low and let wet items dry within 24 to 48 hours.

Renters and shared buildings

If you rent, tell your landlord the same day and keep a written record. Tenants and landlords have different responsibilities depending on the cause, and a written report protects both sides. In a multifamily building, a leak in one unit can create mold in another.

Common household hot spots

  • Window sills and frames where condensation collects in the cold months.
  • Under sinks and behind toilets, where small leaks go unnoticed.
  • Bathroom ceilings above showers with a weak or noisy fan.
  • Closets on exterior walls where air does not move.
  • Laundry rooms and dryer vents that exhaust into the room.
  • Basements and crawl spaces that stay damp through the rainy season.

Prevention beats cleaning

Keep indoor humidity low, since mold and mildew thrive in damp air. A dehumidifier in the basement and a working bathroom fan do more than any spray.

Fix leaks as soon as you find them, clean small spots when they appear and let wet items dry within 24 to 48 hours. Open closet doors now and then so air can move, and keep furniture a few inches off exterior walls.

Check the places you rarely look. Behind the washing machine, inside cabinets under sinks and above the shower are where moisture builds up without notice.

Beyond the small patch

When growth is larger than a small patch, see mold remediation. If a leak caused it, water mitigation comes first.

Common questions

Mildew usually describes flat, powdery growth on surfaces, while mold is often thicker and can penetrate. Both need moisture, and both are cleaned by removing the growth and the moisture.

Some people use vinegar on hard surfaces. Detergent and water with careful scrubbing and drying is the common state-agency approach. Do not mix cleaners.

Porous materials like drywall paper, insulation, ceiling tile and carpet pad that have been wet and moldy are usually replaced.

Multifamily buildings share walls and plumbing, so a leak in one unit can reach another. Report moisture problems early.
