How common are AC leaks in Bothell homes?

.

AC leak water damage in Bothell, WA is less common than in warmer regions, and it is better to say so. The U.S. Energy Information Administration's 2020 survey puts 53 percent of Washington homes as using some air conditioning equipment, with 30 percent using a central unit and 25 percent using an individual unit. Statewide, close to half of homes report none.

Cooling in the houses that have it comes from central units, ductless mini-splits, other individual units and heat pumps. EIA notes that its central category includes central heat pumps and its individual category includes ductless heat pumps. The Washington State Building Code Council states that heat pumps were 21 percent of residential space heating equipment in 2020, with a goal of 39 percent by 2030.

If you have a cooling system and a wet ceiling or floor near it, this page is for you. If not, the more usual Bothell sources are a water heater leak, a failed appliance or a roof leak.

A blocked line can leak slowly for weeks in an attic or closet where nobody looks, and that is what makes these losses expensive.

AC leak safety steps to take first

.
  1. Turn the thermostat to off. A unit with a blocked line will keep adding water.
  2. Switch off power to the unit at its disconnect or breaker if you can reach them dry. Do not touch a unit standing in water.
  3. If a ceiling is stained or bulging, stay out from under it and keep people away.
  4. Photograph the unit, the pan and the damaged area.
  5. Call an HVAC technician for the unit and call us for the water.

Summer in Bothell is dry, which helps diagnosis

.

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Seattle-Tacoma station give July only 0.60 inches of rain. A new ceiling stain in July is therefore unlikely to be a roof leak, and the air handler, a bathroom above or a water heater becomes the first thing to check.

Our process for an AC leak

.

Locate the water

A moisture meter and a thermal camera trace the path from the unit to the wet materials.

Remove standing water

A wet vacuum clears the pan area, the floor and the cavity.

Remove wet material

Soaked insulation, drywall and carpet pad are cut out and bagged.

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run with daily readings until the area is dry.

Hand off

We document the damage. A technician repairs the unit and the line.

Insurance and slow leaks

.

The Washington insurance commissioner says most homeowners policies do not cover gradual leaks that continued for a long time, even if you could not have known, and that insurers consider these maintenance problems. A line that clogs over a season may fall in that category. A sudden overflow is treated differently. Your insurer decides, and we do not promise coverage.

Preventing condensate leaks

.
  • Have the unit serviced and the drain line flushed before the first warm weeks.
  • Change the filter on schedule to avoid coil icing.
  • Ask your technician about a float switch that shuts the unit off when the pan fills.
  • Check the closet or attic floor beneath the unit now and then.
