What a sewage backup is and how it reaches your floor

.

A sewage backup means wastewater from the sanitary sewer or your own drain line is coming up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or shower. The water is Category 3. That means it can carry bacteria and other contaminants, and it is handled differently from a burst pipe.

A blockage stops the flow, and the wastewater rises to the lowest opening in the house, often a basement bath, laundry drain or floor drain.

This page covers sewage. A flooded basement with clean water is on basement water damage cleanup, and flood water from a creek is on flood damage restoration.

Who to call first in Bothell

.

The City of Bothell says several districts operate in and around the city, so your sewer provider depends on your address. The city's utilities page has a find-your-provider link for sewer service. Northshore Utility District serves parts of Bothell. Alderwood Water and Wastewater District lists Canyon Park and North Creek in its service area.

Northshore Utility District says it maintains the sewer in the public right of way, treats piping on private property as the owner's responsibility and says most backups start in private plumbing. Its website lists a 24-hour line for backup emergencies. Call your district first so it can check the main, then a plumber for the private line, then us for cleanup.

What the sources say about sewage in the house

.

The CDC says floodwater may contain sewage and advises washing with soap and clean water as soon as possible and throwing away items that cannot be disinfected, like wall coverings, cloth, rugs and drywall.

Public Health Seattle and King County advises removing wall-to-wall carpet and pad that sewage soaked, replacing saturated or soft plasterboard, keeping children and pets out, wearing rubber gloves and boots and washing hands and laundering clothes afterward.

Source type: the CDC and Public Health Seattle and King County.

Sewage safety: what to do in the first hour

.
  • Stop using water. Do not flush, run the washer or use sinks, because every drain feeds the same line.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with an open wound out of the area.
  • Do not turn power on or off while standing in water. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Call your sewer district, then the 24/7 line. Do not run a shop vacuum on sewage, because it can spray fine droplets.

Signs and what they mean

.

Water gurgles and a drain slows

A blockage is forming. Stop using water and call your district or a plumber before it rises.

Dark water in a floor drain or tub

The line has backed up. Treat it as Category 3 even if the volume is small.

Sewer smell with no visible water

A dry trap or a cracked pipe. Do not ignore it.

What we remove and what we clean

.

Porous materials that sewage soaked cannot be made safe, so they go. That means carpet, pad, soft drywall, insulation and particleboard cabinet bases. Hard, nonporous surfaces like concrete, tile and sealed wood can be cleaned and disinfected.

Protect the crew and the house

Crews wear gloves, boots and protective clothing, and we close doors and cover vents to limit spread.

Extract the liquid

Pumps and extractors remove the water. Waste goes in sealed bags.

Remove porous materials

Carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation are cut out and bagged. A wall is opened back to dry, solid material.

Clean and disinfect

Surfaces are cleaned first because soil blocks a disinfectant. Then an EPA-registered product is applied and kept wet for the contact time on the label.

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure. Readings are logged each visit.

On disinfectants, the EPA says registered products are verified to work according to their label directions and that a surface should stay visibly wet for the whole contact time. It also says registration is not an endorsement of one product over another. We follow the label and make no wider claim.

Insurance and who pays

.

The Washington insurance commissioner lists sewer backup and sump pump overflow among optional endorsements to ask your agent about, so a standard policy may not include them. If the district's main is found at fault, ask the district how it handles claims. We document the damage in photographs and notes for either conversation. Your policy and the facts decide coverage.

Mistakes that make it worse

.
  • Running water to flush the line. It adds to the volume.
  • Letting carpet sit for days. The EPA says to remove what has been wet for more than 24 to 48 hours in flood contamination.
  • Treating it as a drying job only. Contaminated porous material has to come out first. After that, structural drying and dehumidification finishes the job.
