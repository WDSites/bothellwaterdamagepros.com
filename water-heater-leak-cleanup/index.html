When a water heater tank or fitting lets go

.

Water heater leak cleanup covers water that came from the heater itself: a corroded tank, a failed fitting, a stuck relief valve or a loose supply connection. When a tank shell fails, the contents empty quickly and the supply line keeps feeding the leak until the cold valve is closed.

A split pipe unrelated to the tank belongs on burst pipe water damage. If the heater left enough water to pump, emergency water removal explains the extraction.

Where the heater sits decides what gets wet

.

Garage

Water soaks stored boxes and the bottom of the shared wall to the house.

Utility closet

A closet has no drain, so water runs under the door into hallway flooring.

Attic

Water soaks insulation and comes through the ceiling below.

Basement

Water reaches stored goods and the framing of finished walls, and can sit if the drain is clogged.

Why tanks leak: age, anode and relief valve

.

Inside a steel tank, a sacrificial anode rod corrodes so the tank does not. Once the rod is used up, the tank wall itself starts to rust. The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner advises checking the heater for rust, corrosion or moisture, and Seattle Public Utilities lists wet floor spots or a rusted tank as warning signs.

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the safety outlet. If it drips or sprays, pressure or heat is too high or the valve has failed. Have a plumber look at it, and never cap or plug it.

Water heater safety: shut it down in this order

.

Turn off the cold supply

Close the cold water valve above the heater so the tank stops refilling.

Electric unit: breaker off

Switch off the heater's breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Otherwise call your electric utility.

Gas unit: control to pilot or off

Follow the label, or close the quarter-turn gas valve so the handle sits crosswise to the pipe. If you smell gas, leave and call 911.

Watch for hot water

Water from a failed tank or relief valve can scald.

Call a plumber, then us

A plumber repairs or replaces the heater. We handle the water on the floor.

Puget Sound Energy says not to turn your gas back on yourself if it has been shut off, and it takes calls about pilot lights and shut-offs. The Washington Department of Health says power or gas must be off before you open a tank's drain.

Local note.

What Washington's plumbing code says about water heater anchoring

.

A Washington State Building Code Council rule filing for the state's Uniform Plumbing Code amendments says water heaters shall be anchored or strapped to resist horizontal displacement from earthquake motion. It places the straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank's vertical height.

The Department of Health likewise advises securing the top and bottom of the tank with heavy metal straps, not plumbers' tape. Strapping does not prevent a leak, but it keeps a tank from tipping and tearing its connections. Ask the City of Bothell permit center how the current code applies to a replacement.

Source type: State agency rule filings and the Washington Department of Health.

Our process after a heater leak

.

Extract the water

Pumps and extractors clear the floor.

Check what it reached

Moisture meters and a thermal camera find damp drywall, baseboards and flooring edges.

Pull wet materials

Soaked insulation and pad come out, and drywall where it took on water.

Dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry framing and subfloor, with readings logged.

Dry targets follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, cited as a reference and not as a certification. If the leak ran long enough to wet framing, water mitigation describes the full drying process.

Insurance and prevention

.

The Washington insurance commissioner says policies are meant for sudden damage and usually cover those leaks if you report them quickly, but that most do not cover gradual leaks that continued for long. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

To limit damage, add a drain pan under the heater with a leak detector, as the commissioner suggests. Know the location of the cold valve, and keep boxes off the floor around the tank. See our Kenmore and Lynnwood pages for the neighboring cities we dispatch to.
