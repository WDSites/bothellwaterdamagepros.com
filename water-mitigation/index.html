Soaked carpet, a damp wall or a stain that keeps growing?

If carpet squishes underfoot, a wall feels cool and damp, or a ceiling stain keeps spreading, you are describing a mitigation job. Water mitigation is the trade term for stopping the damage during the first hours and days, before it becomes a rebuild. It decides how much of the house can be saved.

Emergency water mitigation in Bothell, WA

Water mitigation in Bothell, WA means shutting off or containing the source, removing standing water, and drying wet framing, subfloors and drywall before they warp or grow mold. If water is moving through your house right now, call the 24/7 line. While you wait, close the main water valve, turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry to reach, and lift valuables off the floor.

Timing is the whole game. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and the Washington Department of Health gives the same window for carpets, clothing, bedding and upholstered furniture. Everything we set up on the first visit is built around that window.

When standing water is an emergency

Any water that is still rising, moving or touching electrical equipment is an emergency. So is water from a toilet, a sewer line or a storm drain, and any leak that has already soaked a ceiling or a wall cavity.

Call 911 or your utility before you call us if you see any of these

  • Water touching a panel, an outlet or a plugged-in appliance.
  • The smell of natural gas, or a gas appliance standing in water.
  • A ceiling that is sagging, bulging or dripping steadily. Stay out from under it.
  • Sewage or floodwater that came from outside the house. Keep children and pets away.

Structural drying: how we dry what you cannot see

Surface water is the easy part. The harder part is the water inside wall cavities, under cabinets and beneath flooring, where you cannot see it. We map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, mark the wet zone and set equipment to match it.

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces. Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so that it does not settle back into the building. In wall cavities and under hardwood we use directed airflow or small openings so that trapped water can leave.

A crew returns to read the meters every day, and equipment is moved, added or removed as the numbers change. Drying is finished when the structure reads dry against an unaffected area of the same material, not when the carpet feels dry. This follows the approach in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Why wet-season Bothell changes the drying plan

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Seattle-Tacoma area, the nearest long-term station, show 39.34 inches of rain a year. November is the wettest month at 6.31 inches, followed by January at 5.78 and December at 5.72. July drops to 0.60. These are regional figures, not Bothell readings, but they explain why damp air is normal in the months when most water losses happen.

Damp outdoor air means that opening windows rarely dries a house in November. Dehumidifiers do the real work, and a crew that places them correctly cuts drying time more than any fan does. Keep exterior doors closed while equipment runs.

Find your shutoff before you need it

The City of Bothell says several water districts operate inside the city and its immediate vicinity, so the utility behind your meter depends on your address. The city's own water comes from Seattle Public Utilities and the Tolt and Cedar watersheds. Ask your provider where the meter is, and find the shutoff inside your house now, while nothing is wrong.

What affects the cost of water mitigation

Every loss is different, so we do not quote a number before we see the damage. These five factors usually drive the bill.

Water category

Clean supply water, gray water and sewage need different handling. Contaminated water adds removal and disinfection.

Affected area

The number of wet rooms and square feet sets the amount of equipment and the days it runs.

Materials involved

Carpet, hardwood, laminate, drywall and plaster dry at different speeds, and some cannot be saved.

Drying days and equipment

More machines for more days costs more. Readings decide the length, not a fixed schedule.

Rebuild scope

Mitigation ends when the building is dry. Replacing drywall, flooring and paint is a separate scope.

Insurance and water mitigation

The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner says home policies are meant for sudden disasters and generally cover water damage that way. It also says coverage can be lost if you delay repairs, so report a loss quickly and follow your insurer's instructions for stopping the leak.

The same agency says that when a policy requires mitigation, insurers have five business days to approve a mitigation scope, and that you may choose your own contractor. We document the damage, the moisture readings and the equipment in writing so that your insurer has what it needs. We do not promise coverage or state what an insurer will pay. Only your policy and your insurer decide that.

What mitigation does not cover

Mitigation is not plumbing repair, waterproofing or rebuilding. A plumber fixes the pipe. A waterproofing contractor handles a foundation that leaks every time it rains. After the structure is dry, water damage repair puts back drywall, flooring and paint.

If the water was dirty, water damage cleanup covers sanitizing and deodorizing. When the water is deep, emergency water removal comes first. Dampness that lingers past the first few days is a question for mold remediation.
